The Story

About the Atlas

01

Built Like a Flight Component

Andrew didn't start with aesthetics — he started with a load specification. Every structural decision in the Atlas exists to protect what's inside. The rigid polymer skeleton, the full-front impact shield, the crush-proof nose bridge — none of it is decorative. This is how an aircraft engineer builds a case.

02

Leather That Earns Its Look

The outer shell is Full-Grain Vachetta leather, sourced and vegetable-tanned in Greece using an ancient process that lets the hide breathe. It arrives pale and unassuming. Over time it deepens into a patina that belongs to no one else. It doesn't wear out. It wears in.

03

For Frames That Don't Fit Anywhere Else

Most hard cases weren't built for larger frames. The Atlas was. If you've ever forced a pair of Wayfarers into a case that was never meant to hold them, you'll understand immediately what this was designed to solve.

04

A Craftsman's Word

The guarantee isn't a policy — it's a personal commitment: "If this shell ever fails you, it returns to my bench in Athens for repair. As long as I am standing at my workbench, I've got your back." No forms, no fine print.
